Method

Two vision-language models read the same photograph and each write what they found in it. Neither returns a number.

What runs

Both are LLaVA-style models with two vision towers. A CLIP tower and a DINOv2 tower read the image; a Vicuna-13B decoder writes the answer as prose. Serving a request holds 26.8 GB of weights resident on the GPU.

Answers run 45 to 62 words. The generation cap is 512 tokens; when a model hits it and stops mid-sentence the response is flagged truncated, and the page says so rather than presenting half a note as finished.

What we changed

LoRA adapters on the released checkpoint: 62,586,880 trainable parameters, 0.799% of the model. Both vision towers and the whole fusion stack were frozen, and are bit-identical before and after training. This is not a model trained from scratch.

What the system does not produce

  • No confidence score. One was built from a logit pass and deliberately deleted for being fragile and unfaithful to the model. There is no meter, gauge or percentage of certainty anywhere on this site.
  • No heatmap. Nothing in the response is spatial — no attention overlay, no bounding box, no per-region score. The model's evidence is its prose.
  • No third verdict. The prediction is only ever real or fake. There is no uncertain state to fall back on.

Results

Fine-tuning did not make the model more accurate on unseen generators. It made the model separate the two classes further apart, and it moved the prior.

The weakness stays on the page. Fake recall fell to 51.7% while real recall reached 100.0%, and the fine-tune answers “real” 70% of the time. A fake verdict from this model is therefore stronger evidence than a real one. That is said next to the verdict itself, not hidden in a footnote.

On quoting these numbers. The accuracy point estimates come from small samples and should not be quoted without that caveat. At n=50 in-distribution, one image is two percentage points; each per-generator cell is four images, so one image is 25 points. Class separation and the shift in the prior are the findings the authors consider robust at this scale.

Paper

Deepfake & AI-Generated Image Detection with Visual Explanation using Vision–Language Models. Abdulqader Alfawzan, Ahmed Sayegh, Muhannad Alfwazan, Marwan Alayed, Ziyad Alghamdi and Salman Khan. KAUST Academy, August 2026.
Read the report (PDF, 2.9 MB)

What it reports

  • Class separation widened from +0.274 to +0.520 — the finding the authors consider robust at this sample size.
  • Out-of-distribution accuracy across ten unseen generators held at 92.5%, unchanged by fine-tuning.
  • The prior moved: the model answers “real” 70% of the time against 46% before, which is what drives real recall to 100% and fake recall down to 51.7%.
  • Trainable parameters 62,586,880, 0.799% of the model, with both vision towers and the fusion stack frozen and verified bit-identical.
