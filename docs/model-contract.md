# Model contract

Measured 2026-08-17 against the live Modal deployment `fakereasoning-inference`. Test image: `frontend/public/_smoke_test.png`, 1536×1024 PNG, itself an AI-generated composite.

## Response shape — this is the whole contract

```json
{
  "prediction": "fake",
  "reasoning": "...",
  "inference_ms": 15558.7,
  "truncated": false,
  "model_repo": "AnnaGao/FakeReasoning",
  "model_revision": "official+fp16",
  "image": { "width": 1536, "height": 1024, "format": "PNG" }
}
```

`prediction` is only ever `"real"` or `"fake"`. There is no confidence field, no heatmap, no bounding boxes, no per-region scores. Nothing spatial exists anywhere in the pipeline.

`model_revision` is `"official+fp16"` or `"finetuned:fakereasoning-finetuned"`.

## Official model — PRIS-CV, FP16 on A100-40GB

Verdict `fake`. 397 characters, 62 words, 4 sentences.

> From low-level perspective: The lighting on the building and the shadows on the objects do not match the overall light source direction.The textures on the building and the surrounding objects appear inconsistent and lack realistic detail. From high-level perspective: The text on the building and the icons are nonsensical and do not form coherent words or symbols. Therefore, this image is fake.

Second run, same image, same verdict, different prose:

> From low-level perspective: The lighting on the building and the objects around it does not match the shadows cast, indicating inconsistency.The textures of the building and surrounding objects appear unnatural and lack realistic detail. From high-level perspective: The image contains a mix of realistic and abstract elements that do not logically fit together, such as the floating icons and incons[...]

Non-deterministic by design: `temperature=0.2` with sampling on, the authors' published config, not overridden. Two visitors uploading the same photograph get different text. Never screenshot this as a canonical answer.

## Fine-tune — 4-bit on A10G

Verdict `fake`. 289 characters, 45 words, 3 sentences.

> The image depicts a modern building with large glass windows, surrounded by palm trees and a clear sky. Despite its seemingly authentic appearance, certain features, such as disproportionate textures or odd lighting, hint that this image was generated by AI. Therefore, this image is fake.

Greedy decoding with a repetition penalty. Reproducible.

## Timing

| | Warm inference | Cold load on top | Worst case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fine-tune | 9.9s | ~63s | ~73s |
| Official | 15.6s | 42.6s (26.8 GB resident) | ~58s |

Both classes scale to zero after 300s idle, so a visitor arriving after a quiet five minutes pays the cold path. Backend timeout is 300s for that reason.

Do not use the word "real-time". "Takes up to a minute" is honest.

## Seven constraints that come from the code, not from taste

1. **No heatmap.** Text only. Would require attention rollout through a LoRA-wrapped LLaVA with a dual CLIP + DINOv2 encoder in 4-bit. Not in scope.
2. **No confidence score.** One was built from a logit pass and deliberately deleted for being fragile and unfaithful to the model. `README.md:305` is stale and still describes it. No meter, no gauge, no percentage.
3. **The verdict appears twice** — once in `prediction`, once as the final sentence of `reasoning` ("Therefore, this image is fake."). The site strips that sentence on render and lets the verdict element carry it. Precedent: `strip_verdict_leak()` does exactly this to the reasoning body during training.
4. **The two models write different shapes.** Official uses labelled halves; the fine-tune is flat prose. A template built around the official structure breaks on the fine-tune.
5. **Answers are short.** 45–62 words. Design for 300–400 characters, tolerate up to ~2000 (512-token cap), and handle `truncated: true` by saying so plainly.
6. **Missing space after some periods** ("direction.The textures"). Backend cleanup collapses whitespace but does not insert missing spaces. The frontend repairs it.
7. **Attribution is required.** Whatever the fine-tune is named, credit PRIS-CV and the base checkpoint `AnnaGao/FakeReasoning` — this is LoRA adapters on their weights, 62.6M parameters, 0.799% of the model, not a model trained from scratch.

## Paper numbers cleared for use on the site

From the KAUST Academy report, 17 August 2026.

| Metric | Base | Fine-tuned |
|---|---|---|
| In-distribution accuracy (n=50) | 64.0% | 72.0% |
| Class separation | +0.274 | +0.520 |
| Out-of-distribution accuracy, 10 unseen generators | 92.5% | 92.5% |
| Recall, real | 61.9% | 100.0% |
| Recall, fake | 65.5% | 51.7% |
| Answers "real" | 46.0% | 70.0% |
| Explanation diversity, in-distribution | 94.0% | 80.0% |

Trainable parameters: 62,586,880 — 0.799% of the model. Both vision towers and the whole fusion stack frozen and bit-identical before and after training.

Per-generator, four images each: DALL·E 3 100%, GigaGAN 100%, GPT-4o 100%, DeepFloyd IF 100%, PixArt 100%, Stable Diffusion 100%, StyleGAN-XL 100%, Flux 75%, Kandinsky 75%, Midjourney 75%.

**Do not quote the accuracy point estimates externally without confidence intervals.** The paper's own limitations section says so: n=50 in-distribution means one image is two percentage points, and the ±25% per-generator cells are one image each. Separation and the prior shift are the findings the authors consider robust at this scale.

**The weakness stays on the page.** Fake recall fell to 51.7% while real recall hit 100%, and the model answers "real" 70% of the time. A fake verdict is therefore stronger evidence than a real one, and the site says so next to the verdict rather than in a footnote.

## Known environment bug

`modal run` on Windows crashes instantly with `'charmap' codec can't encode character '✓'` — the CLI prints a checkmark to a cp1252 console, before anything reaches a GPU. Set `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` and `PYTHONUTF8=1`. Worth adding to the backend README next to the deploy commands.
